# Steward Appointment and Onboarding Protocol

Version 1.0 — Active since ratification of the Steward Eligibility, Orientation, and Appointment Process, July 21, 2026 (decision SD-2026-07-21-01)
Authority: subordinate to the Bridge Builders Constitution, `docs/canonical/CHANGE_AUTHORITY.md`, and `docs/canonical/PERMANENT_STEWARD_POSTS_CHARTER.md` §9.2. This protocol invents no new approval authority; it documents how existing authority is exercised.

## Appointment authority

Determined from existing canonical documents, not created here:

- The **founding steward** remains the appointment decision authority, per Charter §9.2 ("recorded appointing authority per `CHANGE_AUTHORITY.md`") and the authority matrix ("Appoint a steward" — founding-steward approval required). If a future adopted steward decision delegates appointment authority, that decision governs.
- An **occupied Institutional Steward** may review and recommend within delegated authority (verify process followed; consolidate evidence); review is not approval.
- **No candidate may approve their own appointment.** Self-appointment is prohibited (authority matrix).
- **SOPHIA may never approve or appoint.**
- **Completing orientation does not force appointment.** The decision authority may defer, decline, or wait, with reasons recorded.
- **A separate human decision record is always required** — one per appointment, using `STEWARD_APPOINTMENT_DECISION_TEMPLATE.md`.

## The appointment decision

Every appointment decision must include:

- post;
- candidate;
- decision authority;
- date;
- evidence reviewed;
- orientation status;
- readiness finding;
- conflict disclosure;
- recusals;
- appointment scope;
- operating mode;
- access granted;
- access withheld;
- effective date;
- review date;
- continuity-record location;
- acceptance by the appointee (`STEWARD_APPOINTMENT_ACCEPTANCE_TEMPLATE.md` — the decision is not effective without it);
- dissent or reservations.

Every appointment must state:

```text
The occupant is appointed into the post's current Observation-Only Mode.

This appointment grants no authority beyond the ratified Charter, authority
matrix, and current operating protocol.
```

Appointment to a post is not ownership of the post. Appointment does not expand the authority of the post; an occupant receives only the authority already assigned to the post, in its current mode.

## Multiple-post occupancy

One person temporarily occupying more than one post is not prohibited (no existing canon prohibits it), but it is never routine. Any arrangement in which one person may occupy multiple posts requires a separate **concentration-of-authority review**, recorded before or with the second appointment, addressing:

- conflicts between the posts;
- self-review risk;
- recusal requirements;
- approval separation;
- continuity risk;
- succession plan;
- whether the arrangement is temporary;
- how decisions requiring independent review will be routed.

No person may use multiple occupancy to approve their own work. Where the posts' separated roles (proposer / reviewer / approver / auditor) would collapse into one person, the affected decisions route to the founding steward or another independent human reviewer.

## Interim appointments

Interim appointments occur only through a recorded human decision (Charter §9.4), which must state:

- reason;
- restricted scope;
- effective date;
- review date;
- prohibited actions;
- continuity obligations;
- ending condition.

Interim does not mean unaccountable. An interim occupant keeps every record, boundary, and escalation obligation of the post, holds only the authority explicitly recorded, and never more than the post itself holds.

## Access and onboarding

Appointment and system access are separate. Appointment does not automatically create unrestricted access — or any access.

After appointment, an access review covers:

- minimum necessary access;
- repository access;
- private records;
- archive materials;
- participant information;
- publication systems;
- deployment systems;
- governance records;
- administrative systems;
- AI and SOPHIA interfaces.

Every access grant requires:

- least-privilege scoping;
- documented approval;
- a named access owner;
- effective date;
- review date;
- revocation process;
- the appointee's acknowledgment that access is not authority (`STEWARD_ACCESS_AND_AUTHORITY_ACKNOWLEDGMENT_TEMPLATE.md`).

No appointment in Observation-Only Mode automatically receives:

- production deployment access;
- publication authority;
- participant-discipline controls;
- archive approval authority;
- permission-changing authority;
- constitutional editing authority.

Onboarding closes with a continuity record (`STEWARD_ONBOARDING_CONTINUITY_RECORD_TEMPLATE.md`) and a scheduled role review — process stages 13–15.

## Candidate privacy and record handling

Candidate records are governed by `PRIVACY_POLICY.md` and `DATA_RETENTION_POLICY.md` and by these rules:

- collect only necessary information;
- separate public appointment records from private candidate information;
- protect disability and accommodation details;
- protect conflict disclosures;
- avoid unnecessary personal-history collection;
- document who may review candidate records (the decision authority, designated reviewers, and the candidate themselves; others only by recorded need);
- define the record location (private stewardship records storage designated by the decision authority — not public routes, and not this repository's public tree);
- define retention or review needs per `DATA_RETENTION_POLICY.md` review cycles — permanent retention is not invented here; what governance accountability requires to persist is the public appointment record, not the private candidate file;
- allow correction of inaccurate information at the candidate's request;
- preserve required governance evidence without publishing private details.

Never exposed through public routes: candidate applications, accommodation information, unsuccessful-candidacy records, conflict disclosures. Public appointment records contain only what accountability requires: post, occupant, decision authority, date, scope, mode, review date.

## SOPHIA Support During Eligibility, Orientation, and Appointment

SOPHIA may:

- organize source documents;
- generate accessible summaries;
- prepare scenario exercises;
- identify incomplete records;
- compare evidence with stated criteria;
- identify possible conflicts for human review;
- draft orientation questions;
- draft appointment packets;
- identify authority-boundary concerns;
- prepare continuity summaries.

SOPHIA may not:

- recruit people autonomously;
- rank candidates;
- score human worth;
- infer hidden traits;
- diagnose;
- decide eligibility;
- make readiness findings;
- approve orientation completion without human review;
- recommend removal as a final determination;
- appoint;
- reject;
- access private candidate records without authorized access;
- communicate an appointment before a human decision;
- grant access;
- create credentials;
- resolve conflicts of interest;
- act as Institutional Steward;
- replace a human reviewer.

Required notice on SOPHIA-prepared candidate materials:

```text
SOPHIA-prepared material is advisory and administrative support only.
Eligibility, readiness, appointment, access, and governance decisions remain
with authorized human stewards.
```
