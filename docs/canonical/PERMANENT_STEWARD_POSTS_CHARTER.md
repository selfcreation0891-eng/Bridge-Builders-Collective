## Section 1 — Purpose

This Charter creates durable stewardship responsibilities so that orientation, continuity, language, product coherence, and institutional governance do not depend on one person, one session, one application, or one generation. The ecosystem's commitments — informed participation, preserved memory, canonical meaning, honest public claims, accountable human authority — must survive turnover, growth, quiet periods, and time.

The posts exist to preserve:

- participant dignity;
- informed orientation;
- relationship continuity;
- canonical meaning;
- product coherence;
- constitutional integrity;
- accountable human authority;
- intergenerational succession.

The posts are responsibilities of care. They are not status positions, honorifics, or grants of unrestricted administrative power. Holding a post confers exactly the bounded responsibilities and bounded authority this Charter defines — nothing more. Consistent with Constitution §3 ("Stewardship before authority") and §11 ("Open participation does not mean unrestricted authority"), occupying a post is a way of being accountable, not a way of being in charge.

---

## Section 2 — Shared definition of a Permanent Steward Post

A **Permanent Steward Post** is:

- an enduring institutional responsibility;
- occupied by a trained and recorded human steward;
- bounded by delegated authority;
- accountable through records and review;
- transferable through a documented succession process;
- independent of any single person's permanent ownership;
- prohibited from acting outside its mandate.

```text
The post is permanent.
The individual occupying it is not.
```

All five posts established by this Charter are human posts. No AI system, including SOPHIA, may occupy, act as, or substitute for any Permanent Steward Post. A vacant post remains a human post: its responsibilities wait for a human, are covered by the interim procedures in Section 9.4, or are escalated — they are never delegated to an automated system.

## Section 3 — Shared responsibilities of all five posts

Every Permanent Steward Post must:

- observe conditions within its assigned domain;
- preserve relevant context and evidence;
- record material findings;
- distinguish fact, interpretation, recommendation, and decision;
- identify uncertainty rather than convert it into false certainty;
- protect consent and confidentiality per Constitution §5, `PRIVACY_POLICY.md`, and `DATA_RETENTION_POLICY.md`;
- use canonical vocabulary per `CANONICAL_VOCABULARY.md`;
- follow accessibility requirements per `ACCESSIBILITY_STANDARD.md`, including cognitive and emotional accessibility;
- escalate concerns through the routes defined in `docs/stewardship/STEWARD_HANDOFF_AND_ESCALATION_PROTOCOL.md` and `STEWARD_ESCALATION_MATRIX.md`;
- preserve a usable handoff record for every transfer of a concern;
- remain within assigned authority;
- participate in continuity review per `docs/POST_RELEASE_CONTINUITY_PLAN.md` and Section 10 of this Charter;
- disclose conflicts of interest;
- avoid unilateral consequential action during observation-only mode.

## Section 4 — The five Permanent Steward Posts

This Charter establishes five and only five Permanent Steward Posts:

1. Orientation Steward
2. Continuity Steward
3. Vocabulary Steward
4. Product Steward
5. Institutional Steward

### 4.1 Orientation Steward

#### Purpose

Protect informed orientation by identifying where people lack the context necessary to understand what Bridge Builders is, what exists, what does not yet exist, what participation means, and what authority they do or do not have. This post serves the constitutional commitment that "orientation gained, not information delivered" (Constitution §3) and that participation pathways must be truthful about what exists now (Constitution §11).

#### Primary responsibilities

- detect missing context;
- review newcomer understanding (per the "visitor understanding" signal in `docs/POST_RELEASE_CONTINUITY_PLAN.md`);
- identify confusing entry points;
- review onboarding language;
- identify unexplained vocabulary;
- identify misleading participation expectations;
- review cognitive and emotional accessibility of orientation surfaces;
- identify where technical language needs human meaning;
- distinguish orientation from persuasion;
- identify whether a participant can make an informed choice;
- create Rosetta and Vocabulary Steward candidates when wording is unclear.

#### Observation responsibilities

Observe entry points, onboarding pathways, first-contact language, participation-pathway failures, and moments where a person's understanding visibly diverges from what the ecosystem actually is or offers.

#### Required records

Observation records (per template), Rosetta/vocabulary candidates forwarded to the Vocabulary Steward, and handoff records for every concern transferred.

#### Permitted actions

Observe, record, classify, request information, draft recommendations, initiate documented handoffs and escalations, prepare review packets.

#### Actions requiring another steward

Vocabulary candidates require Vocabulary Steward handling; pathway or interface concerns require Product Steward handling.

#### Actions requiring Institutional Steward review

Concerns touching consequential authority, consent, participant safety, or institutional claims.

#### Actions requiring founding-steward approval

Any change to canonical documents, public status, or public claims arising from an orientation finding, per `CHANGE_AUTHORITY.md`.

#### Prohibited actions

The Orientation Steward may not:

- manipulate participation;
- promise outcomes;
- pressure people to continue;
- conceal incomplete states;
- independently rewrite canonical vocabulary;
- approve public claims;
- override accessibility concerns;
- diagnose a participant;
- convert uncertainty into false certainty.

#### Primary handoff relationships

Orientation → Vocabulary (unclear language); Orientation → Product (pathway/interface failures); any-post routes to Continuity and Institutional per the handoff protocol.

#### Review cadence

Weekly observation review; monthly cross-post continuity review; quarterly authority review (Section 10).

#### Succession requirements

Per Section 9.6, including a current role description, active-record index, and usable succession handoff.

### 4.2 Continuity Steward

#### Purpose

Protect relationships, records, institutional memory, handoffs, archival integrity, and operational continuity across sessions, releases, people, and generations. This post serves Constitution §1 and §7 and the continuity practice already established in `docs/POST_RELEASE_CONTINUITY_PLAN.md`.

#### Primary responsibilities

- preserve relationships and material context;
- maintain continuity notes (in `docs/continuity/`, created on first use per the continuity plan);
- identify abandoned decisions and unresolved commitments;
- review handoff completeness;
- protect archive lineage and metadata (Constitution §7);
- track succession readiness;
- maintain decision continuity;
- identify single-person dependencies;
- protect consent history;
- identify contradictions between past and current decisions;
- coordinate continuity certification review per `docs/stewardship/CONTINUITY_CERTIFICATION_STANDARD.md`;
- ensure important context is not lost when a steward rotates out.

#### Observation responsibilities

Observe the state of records, handoffs, succession materials, unresolved commitments, and any place where memory depends on a single person or session.

#### Required records

Continuity notes, observation records, succession-readiness notes, continuity certification review materials, and handoff records.

#### Permitted actions

Observe, record, classify, preserve, request information, draft recommendations, initiate documented handoffs and escalations, coordinate (but not issue) continuity certification review.

#### Actions requiring another steward

Vocabulary contradictions route to the Vocabulary Steward; product-record gaps route to the Product Steward.

#### Actions requiring Institutional Steward review

Unresolved governance decisions, broken succession, authority conflicts, or loss of required accountability revealed by records.

#### Actions requiring founding-steward approval

Any change to a prior adopted decision, canonical record, or archive protection, per `CHANGE_AUTHORITY.md`.

#### Prohibited actions

The Continuity Steward may not:

- change a prior decision without authority;
- rewrite historical records;
- conceal disagreement;
- treat engagement metrics as continuity;
- retain information beyond consent or policy (`DATA_RETENTION_POLICY.md`);
- independently publish archive material;
- reinterpret contributor permissions;
- certify continuity without required evidence and human review.

#### Primary handoff relationships

Continuity → Institutional (governance gaps found in records); receives "any post → Continuity" preservation handoffs from all posts.

#### Review cadence

Weekly observation review; convenes the monthly cross-post continuity review; quarterly authority review (Section 10).

#### Succession requirements

Per Section 9.6. Because this post safeguards succession itself, its own succession materials receive Institutional Steward review at each quarterly authority review.

### 4.3 Vocabulary Steward

#### Purpose

Protect canonical meaning, naming consistency, System Rosetta Stone alignment, public-language clarity, and faithful translation between human meaning and implementation language. This post serves `CANONICAL_VOCABULARY.md` and the System Rosetta Stone's canonical role as the ecosystem's translation layer.

#### Primary responsibilities

- maintain canonical terminology (as recorder and proposer, not approver);
- identify noncanonical or misleading language;
- detect vocabulary drift;
- collect proposed terms and definitions;
- record Rosetta candidates;
- compare public, institutional, technical, and community language;
- preserve user-created terms exactly where canonically protected (for example, **Perspectouence** — never respelled or "corrected");
- flag words that imply unsupported authority, medical status, institutional status, or production readiness (per `PUBLIC_CLAIMS_STANDARD.md`);
- maintain term lineage and rationale;
- prepare vocabulary-change proposals.

#### Observation responsibilities

Observe public copy, documentation, interface labels, community usage, and implementation language for drift from canonical vocabulary.

#### Required records

Observation records, Rosetta candidate lists, term lineage notes, prepared vocabulary-change proposals, and handoff records.

#### Permitted actions

Observe, record, compare, collect, draft proposals, initiate documented handoffs and escalations.

#### Authority boundary

During observation-only mode, the Vocabulary Steward may recommend changes but may not independently alter canonical vocabulary. Vocabulary changes must follow `CHANGE_AUTHORITY.md`: approval belongs to founding steward(s), and to the Rosetta Stone environment stewards once constituted. This Charter does not constitute the Vocabulary Steward as that approval authority.

#### Actions requiring another steward

Orientation-impact findings route to the Orientation Steward; interface-label conflicts route to the Product Steward.

#### Actions requiring Institutional Steward review

Terminology implying unsupported authority, institutional status, clinical status, partnership, readiness, or governance power.

#### Actions requiring founding-steward approval

Every change to `CANONICAL_VOCABULARY.md`, per `CHANGE_AUTHORITY.md`.

#### Prohibited actions

The Vocabulary Steward may not:

- silently rename canonical terms;
- "correct" protected user-created terms;
- approve new vocabulary without required authority;
- change definitions to fit implementation shortcuts;
- use language to exaggerate readiness or outcomes;
- allow SOPHIA to become a vocabulary approval authority.

#### Primary handoff relationships

Vocabulary → Institutional (authority-implying language); receives Orientation → Vocabulary and Product → Vocabulary handoffs.

#### Review cadence

Weekly observation review; monthly cross-post continuity review (vocabulary drift item); quarterly authority review (Section 10).

#### Succession requirements

Per Section 9.6, including the current Rosetta candidate list and term lineage notes.

### 4.4 Product Steward

#### Purpose

Protect coherence between constitutional intent, public experience, product behavior, navigation, accessibility, status declarations, trust notices, and participation pathways. This post serves the binding rule of `ECOSYSTEM_AUTHORITY_ORDER.md`: downstream surfaces consume canonical truth; they never fork it.

#### Primary responsibilities

- review UX coherence;
- identify broken or misleading pathways;
- compare implementation against canonical registry truth (`src/ecosystem/ecosystem-registry.ts`);
- review accessibility evidence (`ACCESSIBILITY_STANDARD.md`, `docs/ACCESSIBILITY_RELEASE_REVIEW.md`);
- identify public-state inconsistencies;
- verify that incomplete environments are honestly labeled per `ENVIRONMENT_STATUS_STANDARD.md`;
- detect dead ends and missing handoffs in participation pathways;
- identify product behavior that pressures or manipulates participants (Constitution §11; `CLAUDE.md` design rules);
- review whether features honor consent and authority boundaries;
- coordinate implementation concerns with relevant technical contributors;
- preserve release evidence and unresolved-product records;
- recommend product corrections.

#### Observation responsibilities

Observe the public front door, generated navigation, status and trust notices, participation pathways, and the relationship between what the registry declares and what the product actually shows.

#### Required records

Observation records, unresolved-product records, release evidence references (`docs/VERIFICATION_EVIDENCE.md` pattern), and handoff records.

#### Permitted actions

Observe, record, compare, verify labels against the status standard, draft recommendations, initiate documented handoffs and escalations.

#### Actions requiring another steward

Meaning and context gaps route to the Orientation Steward; label/language conflicts route to the Vocabulary Steward.

#### Actions requiring Institutional Steward review

Product behavior implicating consent, authority boundaries, safety, or public institutional claims.

#### Actions requiring founding-steward approval

Public environment status changes and public-claim changes, per `CHANGE_AUTHORITY.md`; implementation merges follow the pull-request process (steward approval, CI green) and are not granted to this post by this Charter.

#### Prohibited actions

The Product Steward may not:

- merge or deploy merely because the role identified a concern;
- change canonical truth to match a downstream implementation error (`CHANGE_AUTHORITY.md`: the registry is never edited to match a downstream error);
- approve its own consequential implementation change;
- bypass CI, accessibility, security, or human review;
- represent a preview as complete;
- use engagement optimization against constitutional principles;
- authorize SOPHIA to modify production systems.

#### Primary handoff relationships

Product → Orientation (pathways lacking human meaning); Product → Vocabulary (label conflicts with canon); receives Orientation → Product handoffs.

#### Review cadence

Weekly observation review; monthly cross-post continuity review (product-coherence item); quarterly authority review (Section 10).

#### Succession requirements

Per Section 9.6, including the unresolved-product record index and current release evidence locations.

### 4.5 Institutional Steward

#### Purpose

Protect constitutional integrity, accountable governance, authority boundaries, institutional claims, documented decisions, escalation fairness, and the proper exercise of human stewardship.

This is a **human stewardship post**. SOPHIA must never occupy this post. No automated system may exercise, simulate, or stand in for its judgment.

Holding this post does not confer constitutional authority. The Institutional Steward reviews, routes, and records; constitutional interpretation, amendment, ratification, and the approvals reserved in `CHANGE_AUTHORITY.md` remain with founding steward(s) and the processes already defined in canonical documents.

#### Primary responsibilities

- review governance conformity;
- preserve authority boundaries;
- confirm that required decision records exist;
- review escalated cross-post concerns;
- identify constitutional conflicts;
- review institutional and partnership claims against `PUBLIC_CLAIMS_STANDARD.md`;
- verify that appointments and removals follow this Charter;
- maintain conflict-of-interest disclosures;
- coordinate steward decision records;
- distinguish recommendation from adopted decision;
- route constitutional matters to founding stewards;
- ensure consequential decisions remain human (Constitution §8);
- oversee the integrity of the ratification process without self-ratifying.

#### Observation responsibilities

Observe governance practice: whether decisions are recorded, whether authority stays within bounds, whether escalations are handled fairly, and whether public institutional claims carry required evidence.

#### Required records

Observation records, decision-record index, conflict-of-interest disclosure records, escalation review notes, and handoff records.

#### Permitted actions

Observe, record, review, route, request information, draft recommendations, convene reviews, initiate documented escalation to founding steward(s).

#### Actions requiring another steward

Continuity-record gaps route to the Continuity Steward; language findings route to the Vocabulary Steward.

#### Actions requiring Institutional Steward review

Not applicable to itself; where the Institutional Steward is the subject of a concern, review routes directly to founding steward(s).

#### Actions requiring founding-steward approval

Constitutional interpretation and amendment; ratification; Charter amendment; reserved appointments and removals; major public institutional claims — per `CHANGE_AUTHORITY.md` and Constitution §13.

#### Prohibited actions

The Institutional Steward may not:

- override the Constitution;
- approve constitutional amendments unless independently authorized as a founding steward;
- grant itself unlimited authority;
- use SOPHIA as a proxy decision-maker;
- conceal dissent;
- combine proposer, reviewer, approver, and auditor roles where separation is required;
- discipline or remove a steward without documented process (Section 9.5);
- convert institutional interest into a public partnership claim without evidence (`PUBLIC_CLAIMS_STANDARD.md`).

#### Primary handoff relationships

Institutional → Founding Steward (matters exceeding delegated authority); receives Vocabulary → Institutional, Continuity → Institutional, and "any post → Institutional" handoffs.

#### Review cadence

Weekly observation review; monthly cross-post continuity review; convenes the quarterly authority review (Section 10).

#### Succession requirements

Per Section 9.6. Succession of this post is reviewed by founding steward(s), not by the post itself.

## SOPHIA Advisory Relationship

SOPHIA is an advisory presence, constitutionally bounded by Constitution §9 and restated in `CHANGE_AUTHORITY.md`. SOPHIA supports all five Permanent Steward Posts. SOPHIA occupies none of them.

SOPHIA may support every post by:

- identifying missing context;
- comparing records;
- detecting contradictions;
- surfacing unresolved items;
- drafting summaries;
- drafting recommendation options;
- identifying possible canonical conflicts;
- organizing evidence;
- preparing handoff packets;
- identifying questions requiring human judgment;
- assisting with continuity review.

SOPHIA may not:

- occupy a steward post;
- vote;
- appoint or remove stewards;
- approve or ratify decisions;
- publish;
- merge;
- deploy;
- change canonical records;
- change permissions;
- discipline participants;
- diagnose;
- certify continuity;
- close an escalation;
- make final determinations;
- communicate a recommendation as an adopted decision.

Every SOPHIA-generated artifact supporting a steward post must distinguish:

```text
Observed evidence
Detected pattern
Uncertainty
Advisory interpretation
Recommended human review
Human decision status
```

Required notice on SOPHIA-generated artifacts:

```text
SOPHIA advisory output is not an adopted steward decision. Human review and recorded authority are required before consequential action.
```

Any representation of SOPHIA as a steward, approval authority, autonomous actor, or substitute for human judgment violates the Constitution and must be recorded and corrected per `CHANGE_AUTHORITY.md`.

## Section 9 — Appointment, rotation, temporary absence, removal, and succession

### 9.1 Eligibility

A steward must demonstrate:

- understanding of the Constitution;
- role-specific orientation;
- consent and confidentiality awareness;
- accessibility awareness;
- ability to distinguish evidence from interpretation;
- ability to document decisions and uncertainty;
- willingness to remain accountable;
- disclosure of material conflicts of interest.

Eligibility is demonstrated through orientation and review, not credentials. No degree, professional license, or employment status is required by this Charter.

### 9.2 Appointment

Appointment to a post requires:

- nomination or application;
- role-specific orientation;
- review against the eligibility requirements in Section 9.1;
- documented conflict disclosure;
- defined scope;
- recorded appointing authority (per `CHANGE_AUTHORITY.md`);
- effective date;
- observation-only starting status;
- designated review date;
- continuity record.

This Charter does not determine who currently occupies any post. Occupancy is established only by separate, recorded appointment decisions. Until such a decision exists for a post, the post is vacant.

The detailed candidate, eligibility, orientation, readiness, appointment, and onboarding process elaborating this section is `docs/stewardship/STEWARD_ELIGIBILITY_ORIENTATION_AND_APPOINTMENT_PROCESS.md` (pending ratification; operative only once ratified per `CHANGE_AUTHORITY.md`). That process is subordinate to this Charter and adds no appointment authority.

### 9.3 Rotation

Rotation is a continuity practice, not automatic removal. Rotation requires:

- advance notice where feasible;
- complete succession handoff (per the succession handoff template);
- access review;
- unresolved-work transfer;
- relationship-context transfer;
- incoming steward acknowledgment;
- continuity review.

This Charter sets no arbitrary term limits; no existing canonical authority requires them. Term limits, if ever adopted, enter through `CHANGE_AUTHORITY.md`.

### 9.4 Temporary absence

For a temporary absence, the following apply:

- **temporary delegation** — specific responsibilities may be delegated to another serving steward for a defined period, recorded with scope and end date;
- **interim steward designation** — an interim steward may be designated by the appointing authority, recorded like an appointment, with observation-only status regardless of the post's current mode;
- **restricted authority** — an interim steward holds only the authority explicitly recorded, never more than the absent steward held;
- **return-to-role review** — the returning steward and the interim steward complete a documented review of what occurred during the absence;
- **preservation of unresolved matters** — open observations, handoffs, and escalations are preserved and transferred, never closed by the absence itself.

### 9.5 Removal

Grounds for removal may include:

- misuse of authority;
- confidentiality breach;
- discriminatory conduct;
- repeated failure to maintain required records;
- serious consent violation;
- undisclosed conflict of interest;
- constitutional or governance violation;
- abandonment of the post without continuity handoff.

Removal requires:

- recorded concern;
- notice to the steward where safety allows;
- evidence review;
- opportunity to respond where appropriate;
- conflict-of-interest review of those deciding;
- authorized human decision, recorded per `CHANGE_AUTHORITY.md`;
- continuity and access transfer;
- written outcome;
- preservation of dissent or appeal information where applicable.

SOPHIA may organize evidence for a removal review. SOPHIA may not recommend removal as a final determination, may not decide removal, and may not execute removal.

### 9.6 Succession

Every post must maintain:

- a current role description;
- an active-record index;
- an access inventory;
- an open-issue list;
- a record of primary relationships;
- known risks;
- an interim continuity procedure;
- a completed-or-ready succession handoff template;
- at least one identified process for emergency continuity.

Naming a permanent successor is not required before ratification. Succession readiness is reviewed through the continuity certification standard (`docs/stewardship/CONTINUITY_CERTIFICATION_STANDARD.md`).

## Section 10 — Review cadence

The minimum operating cadence is deliberately small. It creates visibility, not meetings.

### Weekly observation review

Each active post reviews its domain and records only material findings, aligned with the weekly operating cycle in `docs/POST_RELEASE_CONTINUITY_PLAN.md`.

### Monthly cross-post continuity review

Convened by the Continuity Steward. Reviews:

- unresolved handoffs;
- repeated orientation failures;
- vocabulary drift;
- product-coherence issues;
- governance conflicts;
- succession risks;
- SOPHIA advisory-boundary issues.

### Quarterly authority review

Convened by the Institutional Steward. Reviews:

- whether delegated authority remains appropriate;
- whether any post has accumulated excessive control;
- whether observation-only status should remain;
- whether records and succession materials remain usable;
- whether institutional or public claims remain truthful.

### Event-triggered review

Required after:

- serious safety or consent concerns;
- a major release;
- a change in public status;
- a steward appointment or departure;
- an authority conflict;
- a material archive incident;
- a constitutional amendment;
- a major partnership or institutional claim;
- evidence that SOPHIA exceeded or was represented beyond its advisory boundary.
