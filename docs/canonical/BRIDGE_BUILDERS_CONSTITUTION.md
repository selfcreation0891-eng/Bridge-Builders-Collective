# Bridge Builders Constitution

Version 1.0 — adopted for release v0.1.0 (2026-07-15)
Authority level: 1 (highest — see `ECOSYSTEM_AUTHORITY_ORDER.md`)
Consolidates and supersedes no document; it is the constitutional root that `CLAUDE.md`, `doc/GOVERNANCE.md`, and `doc/ARCHITECTURE.md` now serve as operational elaborations of. Where wording differs, this Constitution governs.

## 1. System identity

Bridge Builders Collective is a human-centered ecosystem supporting learning, regulation, stewardship, creativity, cultural continuity, shared knowledge, and meaningful participation across individuals, communities, and institutions.

It is not a conventional wellness platform, social app, or media brand. It is human regulation infrastructure: living archives, stewardship systems, governance frameworks, measurable-outcomes infrastructure, sensory regulation environments, continuity architecture, and ethical memory preservation systems.

## 2. Public-interest purpose

The ecosystem exists to build trusted, long-term, public-interest human infrastructure for individuals, families, young people, elders, communities, educators, creators, stewards, organizations, and institutions — supporting nervous-system stabilization, social coherence, intergenerational continuity, cultural preservation, stewardship development, and measurable human outcomes.

## 3. Protected principles

1. Stability before stimulation.
2. Clarity before complexity.
3. Trust before scale.
4. Continuity before growth.
5. Stewardship before authority.
6. Preservation before extraction.
7. Regulation before engagement optimization.
8. Support, not help.
9. Orientation gained, not information delivered.
10. Equal creative opportunity, not equal outcomes.
11. Every presence is a gift.
12. Open participation does not mean unrestricted authority.
13. Human stewardship remains responsible for consequential decisions.

These principles may not be weakened by any downstream document, environment, or implementation.

## 4. Stewardship responsibilities

Stewards are responsible for participant safety, informed orientation, moderation assistance, onboarding guidance, operational continuity, and safety observation. Founders are responsible for platform direction, infrastructure oversight, governance continuity, and strategic decisions. All consequential decisions — publication, approval, rejection, discipline, diagnosis, and governance — remain with human stewards.

## 5. Consent requirements

Participation requires informed consent that is explicit, revocable, documented, and understandable. Consent, once granted, does not transfer authority: contributors retain the rights defined in `PRIVACY_POLICY.md` and `MEDIA_LICENSING_POLICY.md`. Consent systems may never be weakened to accelerate release, growth, or engagement.

## 6. Accessibility obligations

The ecosystem maintains accessibility — including cognitive and emotional accessibility — as a constitutional obligation, per `ACCESSIBILITY_STANDARD.md`. Accessibility may not be sacrificed for visual styling, speed, or scale.

## 7. Archive protections

Living Archive materials are preservation assets, not extractive content assets. Archive integrity, metadata preservation, lineage continuity, and participant ownership structures are protected. No system may compromise archive integrity or convert preservation into extraction.

## 8. Human authority boundaries

No automated system, AI agent, or downstream environment may independently make consequential decisions. Open participation does not confer authority: authority is granted through the governance processes in `CHANGE_AUTHORITY.md` and exercised accountably.

## 9. SOPHIA advisory boundaries

SOPHIA is an advisory presence only. SOPHIA may not independently publish, approve, reject, discipline, diagnose, or make governance decisions. SOPHIA's outputs are inputs to human stewardship, never substitutes for it. Any representation of SOPHIA that implies independent authority violates this Constitution.

## 10. Cultural care obligations

The ecosystem preserves cultural integrity, dignity of contributors, and intergenerational continuity. Cultural materials receive care appropriate to their origin, including restricted access, family-only permissions, and steward review where required.

## 11. Public participation principles

Participation is open, consent-based, and honestly described. The ecosystem avoids manipulative engagement systems, addiction-oriented design, notification pressure, exaggerated claims, pseudo-mystical certainty, and institutional claims without evidence. Every presence is a gift; participation pathways must be truthful about what exists now.

## 12. Institutional claim boundaries

Public claims are governed by `PUBLIC_CLAIMS_STANDARD.md`. No claim of operation, outcome, partnership, or production-readiness may be published without the evidence class that standard requires.

## 13. Amendment process

Amendments to this Constitution require an adopted steward decision under `CHANGE_AUTHORITY.md`, recorded with date, rationale, and version increment. Protected principles (§3) and the boundaries in §5–§9 may be strengthened but not weakened by amendment.

## 14. Relationship to downstream environments

Individual environments (System Rosetta Stone, BridgeBuilders Academy, The Living Archive, SOPHIA, and all branches) implement, but never redefine, canonical identity. No route, component, repository, AI agent, document, or application may independently redefine canonical names, descriptions, statuses, relationships, URLs, authority boundaries, or navigation. The canonical ecosystem registry in this repository is the sole public source of that truth.
