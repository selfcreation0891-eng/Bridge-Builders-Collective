This document defines steward responsibilities, escalation authority, and operational response hierarchy.

Cross-post handoff and escalation routes for the five Permanent Steward Posts are defined in `docs/stewardship/STEWARD_HANDOFF_AND_ESCALATION_PROTOCOL.md` (active since the Charter's ratification, July 20, 2026; posts are observation-only and vacant until appointed). The routes say *where* a concern goes; the tiers below say *how urgently*. The tiers below are unchanged by that protocol.

---

# 1. Steward Principles

Stewards are expected to prioritize:

- participant safety
- emotional regulation
- accountability
- continuity integrity
- respectful communication
- governance compliance

---

# 2. Escalation Structure

## Tier 1 — Observation

Examples:
- usability confusion
- non-harmful disputes
- onboarding issues

Action:
- document
- assist
- monitor

---

## Tier 2 — Moderation Concern

Examples:
- repeated disruptive behavior
- consent boundary violations
- harmful communication

Action:
- moderation review
- temporary restrictions
- escalation logging

---

## Tier 3 — Serious Safety Risk

Examples:
- threats
- exploitation
- targeted harassment
- severe governance violations

Action:
- immediate escalation
- access suspension
- incident response activation

---

## Tier 4 — Emergency

Examples:
- credible threats of harm
- illegal activity
- severe platform compromise

Action:
- emergency escalation
- legal consultation where required
- full administrative review

---

# 3. Documentation Requirements

All escalations should include:

- timestamps
- steward notes
- involved systems
- actions taken
- outcome status

---

# 4. Steward Accountability

Stewards may be reviewed for:

- misuse of authority
- confidentiality breaches
- discriminatory conduct
- governance violations
